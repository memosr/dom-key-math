The Throne is the only real lever

Pool size counts twice in the Throne formula, so returns climb in $250 steps and stay at zero below it.

Careful with this one. Snapshot numbers, not a promise. Funded by 5c a key, a pool only reaches $2,500 after a long unclaimed stretch, so high readings are rare, not average.

Does the model hold together

Do the funded shares actually add up to what the designers promised?

Blessings overshoot their funding: 21.1c comes out per key against 18c put in, a gap covered by every run that dies before floor 10. Balance sits at an 85% clear rate.

The Throne undershoots, then overshoots: $407 returns 1.6c against 5c of funding, break-even lands at exactly $625.

Add the designed shares and you get 93c. That's true only by coincidence, when the Blessing surplus cancels the Throne shortfall at small pools.

What this model cannot tell you

Four gaps, left open rather than guessed.

  • Whether the Weekly pool is seededUnstated. DoM 1.0 seeded $5,000 once, so it's plausible, and would skew any pool-size read on sales.
  • What happens when the Blessing Pool runs thinDoM 1.0 had a fix; 2.0 drops it unreplaced, leaving a 21x cut against a shallow pool undocumented.
  • The real clear rateUnpublished, though it's the model's most critical input. The dial above is a guess until the team shares it.
  • Buba CapsListed in the Arcade header with Treasure, Blessings, and Raffle Tickets. Not mentioned on the whitepaper's rewards page.
